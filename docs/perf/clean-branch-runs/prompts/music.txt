Equal temperament divides the octave into twelve logarithmically equal semitones, each a ratio of the twelfth root of two, roughly 1.0595. The compromise is that no interval except the octave is acoustically pure: the fifth is about two cents narrow and the major third about fourteen cents wide against just intonation. Baroque keyboard players used well temperaments instead, which distributed the comma unevenly so that commonly used keys sounded nearly pure while remote keys acquired a distinctive colour. That colouring is why keys carried characters in eighteenth century writing, and why the practice of transposing a piece could genuinely change its affect. Modern equal temperament erases those differences, which is a gain in flexibility and a real loss in expressive vocabulary.

Turbidite sequences record submarine density flows and follow the Bouma sequence: a graded basal division, then parallel lamination, then ripple cross-lamination, then upper parallel lamination, then a pelagic cap. A complete sequence is uncommon; most beds preserve only some divisions, and which ones survive depends on distance from source and on erosion by subsequent flows. Correlating them across a basin therefore requires care, because the absence of a division carries information but is easily mistaken for a different depositional environment. The Annot Sandstone in southeastern France and the Marnoso-Arenacea in the Apennines are the classic field examples, and both have been remapped several times as the interpretation of amalgamated beds has changed.

A properly made hollandaise depends on holding an emulsion of butterfat in a water phase supplied by egg yolk and a small quantity of acid. The yolk contributes lecithin and lipoproteins that coat the fat droplets, and the acid both flavours the sauce and lowers the pH enough to stabilise those proteins against premature coagulation. Temperature control matters more than technique: above roughly eighty-five degrees the yolk proteins set and the emulsion breaks into a grainy suspension. Rescuing a broken sauce means starting a fresh yolk with a spoonful of water and whisking the broken mixture into it slowly, which rebuilds the continuous phase around the fat that has already separated.

Container shipping schedules are built around string rotations rather than point to point voyages. A vessel on an Asia to Northern Europe string calls at five or six Asian ports, transits Suez, calls at three or four European ports, and returns, with the whole loop taking between ten and twelve weeks. Because the loop length is fixed by the schedule, a carrier maintains as many vessels as there are weeks in the rotation to offer weekly departures from every port. Delays propagate around the loop rather than dissipating, so a single congested berth in Europe shows up as a missed sailing in Asia two months later, and the standard mitigation is to skip a port call and reposition rather than to attempt recovery within the rotation.
