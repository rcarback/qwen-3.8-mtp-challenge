## Narrative

Total if all land: about 1.67 ms per token, or 2.7 percent of the 61 ms step, taking the conservative RoPE figure. Two pairs overlap and are counted once each. Levers 2 and 3 in the source list are the same work seen twice (hoist the cos/sin table versus memoise it), so the table carries one row at 0.25 to 0.50 ms; the upper end gives a 1.92 ms total. Lever 5 sits inside the pre-budget path that lever 3 removes, so its 0.001 ms is inside lever 3, not added to it. Lever 4 pays on the draft step, not on the serial decode step, so it does not stack cleanly with the rest.

Where the 3.5x gap comes from, in mechanism terms: not bytes, but the number of separately dispatched matmuls per layer and the dependency gaps between them. Our own measurement puts about 33 ms of the step in that launch-and-gap term across 60 to 100 MLX operations per layer over 48 layers, and about 31 ms in dense weight streaming. Their forward collapses each layer into a handful of hand-written Metal kernels, so each remaining launch is a matmul with no gap in front of it. Our compile pass already took the elementwise glue, which is exactly why the surviving levers are small. What is left is matmul-to-matmul, and only a kernel that removes a matmul dispatch closes it. Part of the gap is also a different weight pack on different silicon, not architecture.

Total if every surviving lever lands: about 1.67 ms per token.
